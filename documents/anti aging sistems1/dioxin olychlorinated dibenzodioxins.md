General structure of PCDDs where *n* and *m* can range from 0 to 4

**Polychlorinated dibenzodioxins** (**PCDD**s), or simply **dioxins**, are a group of [polyhalogenated compounds](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhalogenated_compound) which are significant because they act as environmental [pollutants](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutant). They are commonly referred to as dioxins for simplicity in scientific publications because every PCDD [molecule](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) contains a [dioxin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin_%28chemical%29) skeletal structure. Typically, the *p*-dioxin skeleton is at the core of a PCDD molecule, giving the molecule a [dibenzo-*p*-dioxin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibenzodioxin) ring system. Members of the PCDD family have been shown to [bioaccumulate](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioaccumulate) in humans and [wildlife](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife) due to their [lipophilic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipophilic) properties, and are known [teratogens](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratology), [mutagens](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutagen), and suspected human [carcinogens](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen). They are [organic compounds](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compounds).

Dioxins occur as by-products in the manufacture of [organochlorides](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organochloride), in the incineration of chlorine-containing substances such as [PVC](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PVC) (polyvinyl chloride), in the bleaching of paper, and from natural sources such as volcanoes and forest fires.[^\[1\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-beychok1987-0) There have been many incidents of dioxin pollution resulting from industrial emissions and accidents; the earliest such incidents were in the mid 18th century during the [Industrial Revolution](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^\[2\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-weber2008-1)

The word \"dioxins\" may also refer to a similar but unrelated compound, the polychlorinated [dibenzofurans](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibenzofurans) (PCDFs) of like environmental importance.

The [skeletal formula](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_formula) and [substituent](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituent) numbering scheme of the parent compound dibenzo-*p*-dioxin

The structure of dibenzo-*p*-dioxin comprises two [benzene rings](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene_ring) joined by two oxygen bridges. This makes the compound an [aromatic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic) di[ether](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ether). The name [dioxin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin) formally refers to the central dioxygenated ring, which is stabilized by the two flanking [benzene rings](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene_ring).

In PCDDs, chlorine atoms are attached to this structure at any of 8 different places on the molecule, at positions 1--4 and 6--9. There are 75 different types of PCDD [congeners](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congener) (that is: related dioxin compounds).[^\[3\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-2) The toxicity of PCDDs depends on the number and positions of the chlorine atoms. [Congeners](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congener) that have chlorines in the 2, 3, 7, and 8 positions have been found to be significantly toxic. In fact, 7 congeners have chlorine atoms in the relevant positions which were considered toxic by the [NATO](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) *Committee on the Challenges to Modern Society* (NATO/CCMS) international toxic equivalent (I-TEQ) scheme.^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=2)\] Historical perspective
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

![](media/image5.png){width="2.2916666666666665in" height="0.8645833333333334in"}

![](media/image2.png){width="0.15555555555555556in" height="0.11458333333333333in"}

Structure of *2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin* (TCDD)

Low concentrations of dioxins existed in nature prior to [industrialization](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) due to natural combustion and geological processes.^[\[4\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-3)[\[5\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-4)^ Dioxins were first unintentionally produced as by-products from 1848 onwards as [Leblanc process](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leblanc_process) plants started operating in Germany.[^\[2\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-weber2008-1) The first intentional synthesis of chlorinated dibenzodioxin was in 1872. Today, concentrations of dioxins are found in all humans, with higher levels commonly found in persons living in more industrialized countries. The most toxic dioxin, **2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzo-*p*-dioxin** (TCDD), became well known as a contaminant of [Agent Orange](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange), an herbicide used in the Vietnam War.[^\[6\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16445906-5) Later, dioxins were found in [Times Beach](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Beach), [Missouri](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri)[^\[7\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-6) and [Love Canal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Canal), [New York](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York)[^\[8\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-7) and [Seveso](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seveso), [Italy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy).[^\[9\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-8) More recently, dioxins have been in the news with the poisoning of President [Viktor Yushchenko](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko) of [Ukraine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) in 2004,[^\[10\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-nature.com-9) the Naples Mozzarella Crisis[^\[11\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-independent.co.uk-10) and the [Irish pork crisis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_pork_crisis) of 2008.

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=3)\] Sources of dioxins
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The [United States Environmental Protection Agency](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency) [Dioxin Reassessment Report](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin_Reassessment_Report) is possibly the most comprehensive review of dioxins, but other countries now have substantial research. [Australia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), [New Zealand](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) and the [United Kingdom](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) all have substantial research into [body burdens](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_burden) and sources. Tolerable daily, monthly or annual intakes have been set by the [World Health Organization](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) and a number of governments. Dioxins enter the general population almost exclusively from ingestion of food, specifically through the consumption of fish, meat, and dairy products since dioxins are fat-soluble and readily climb the [food chain](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_chain).[^\[12\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid11346131-11)

![](media/image6.png){width="1.875in" height="2.8125in"}

![](media/image2.png){width="0.2111111111111111in" height="0.15486111111111112in"}

Concentration profile of PCDD in a dated sediment core from [Esthwaite Water](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esthwaite_Water), [Cumbria](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria), [UK](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK).

Occupational exposure is an issue for some in the chemical industry, or in the application of chemicals, notably [herbicides](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbicide). Inhalation has been a problem for people living near substantial point sources where emissions are not adequately controlled. In many developed nations there are now emissions regulations which have alleviated some concerns, although the lack of continuous sampling of dioxin emissions causes concern about the understatement of emissions. In [Belgium](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium), through the introduction of a process called [AMESA](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMESA), continuous sampling showed that periodic sampling understated emissions by a factor of 30 to 50 times. Few facilities have continuous sampling.

Most controversial is the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency) (US EPA) assessment\'s (draft) finding that any [reference dose](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_dose) that were to be set would be far below current average intakes.

Children are passed substantial body burdens by their mothers, and [breastfeeding](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breastfeeding) increases the child\'s body burden.Przyrembel H, Heinrich-Hirsch B, Vieth B (2000). \"Exposition to and health effects of residues in human milk.\". *Adv. Exp. Med. Biol.* **478**: 307--25. [doi](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_object_identifier):[10.1007/0-306-46830-1\_27](http://dx.doi.org/10.1007%2F0-306-46830-1_27). [PMID 11065082](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/11065082). \</ref\> Children\'s body burdens are often many times above the amount implied by tolerable intakes which are based on body weight. Breast fed children usually have substantially higher dioxin body burdens than non breast fed children until they are about 8 to 10 years old. The [WHO](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) still recommends breast feeding for its other benefits.[^\[13\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-12)

Dioxins are produced in small concentrations when organic material is burned in the presence of [chlorine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine), whether the chlorine is present as [chloride](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloride) [ions](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) or as [organochlorine compounds](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organochlorine_compound), so they are widely produced in many contexts. According to the most recent US EPA data, the major sources of dioxins are:

-   Coal fired utilities

-   Municipal waste incinerators[^\[1\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-beychok1987-0)

-   Metal smelting

-   Diesel trucks

-   Land application of sewage sludge

-   Burning treated wood

-   Trash burn barrels

These sources together account for nearly 80% of dioxin emissions.

When the original US EPA inventory of dioxin sources was done in 1987, incineration represented over 80% of known dioxin sources. As a result, US EPA implemented new emissions requirements. These regulations have been very successful in reducing dioxin stack emissions from incinerators. Incineration of municipal solid waste, medical waste, sewage sludge, and hazardous waste together now produce less than 3% of all dioxin emissions.^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^

In incineration, dioxins can also reform or form de novo in the [atmosphere](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_atmosphere) above the stack as the exhaust gases cool through a temperature window of 600 to 200 °C. The most common method of reducing the quantity of dioxins reforming or forming de novo is through rapid (30 millisecond) [quenching](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quenching) of the exhaust gases through that 400 °C window.[^\[14\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid17410797-13) Incinerator emissions of dioxins have been reduced by over 90% as a result of new emissions control requirements. Incineration in developed countries is now a very minor contributor to dioxin emissions.

![](media/image2.png){width="0.2111111111111111in" height="0.15486111111111112in"}

A chart illustrating how much dioxin the average American consumes per day. (Note: pg = picogram, or one trillionth of a gram, or 10^−12^ g) [^\[12\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid11346131-11).

Dioxins are also generated in reactions that do not involve burning --- such as bleaching fibers for paper or textiles, and in the manufacture of chlorinated phenols, particularly when reaction temperature is not well controlled. Affected compounds include the wood preservative [pentachlorophenol](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentachlorophenol), and also [herbicides](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbicide) such as [2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,4-D) (or 2,4-D) and [2,4,5-trichlorophenoxyacetic acid](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,4,5-trichlorophenoxyacetic_acid) (2,4,5-T). Higher levels of chlorination require higher reaction temperatures and greater dioxin production. See [Agent Orange](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange) for more on contamination problems in the 1960s. Dioxins may also be formed during the [photochemical](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemical) breakdown of the common antimicrobial compound [triclosan](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triclosan).[^\[15\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid15779749-14)

Dioxins are also in typical [cigarette smoke](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_constituents).[^\[16\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-15) Dioxin in cigarette smoke was noted as \"understudied\" by the US EPA in its \"Re-Evaluating Dioxin\" (1995). In that same document, the US EPA acknowledged that dioxin in cigarettes is \"anthropogenic\" (man-made, \"not likely in nature\"). Nevertheless, the use of chlorine-containing tobacco [pesticides](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticide) and chlorine-bleached cigarette papers remains legal^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^.

Dioxins are present in minuscule amounts in a wide range of materials used by humans --- including practically all substances manufactured using [plastics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic), [resins](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin), or [bleaches](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleach).^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^ Such materials include [tampons](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampon), and a wide variety of food packaging substances.^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^ The use of these materials means that all Western humans receive at least a *very* small daily dose of dioxin^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^---however, it is disputed whether such exceptionally tiny exposures have any clinical relevance^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^. It is even controversially discussed whether dioxins might have a non-linear dose-response curve with beneficial health effects in a certain lower dose range, a phenomenon called [hormesis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormesis).[^\[17\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-16)

Dietary sources of dioxin in the United States have been analyzed by the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency) (EPA) and scientists from other organizations.

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=4)\] Toxicity
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dioxins are absorbed primarily through dietary intake of fat, as this is where they accumulate in animals and humans. In humans, the highly chlorinated dioxins are stored in fatty tissues and are neither readily metabolized nor excreted. The estimated elimination [half-life](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) for highly chlorinated dioxins (4-8 chlorine atoms) in humans ranges from 7.8 to 132 years.[^\[18\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid12143938-17)

The persistence of a particular dioxin congener in an animal is thought to be a consequence of its structure. It is believed that dioxins with few chlorines, which thus contain hydrogen atoms on adjacent pairs of carbons, can more readily be oxidized by [cytochromes P450](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytochrome_P450).^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^ The oxidized dioxins can then be more readily excreted rather than stored for a long time.^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^

![](media/image8.png){width="2.2916666666666665in" height="1.2916666666666667in"}

![](media/image2.png){width="0.15555555555555556in" height="0.11458333333333333in"}

[Space-filling model](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-filling_model) of 2,3,7,8- tetrachlorodibenzo-*p*-dioxin.

2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzo-*p*-dioxin (TCDD) is the most toxic of the [congeners](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congener). Other dioxin congeners (or mixtures thereof) are given a toxicity rating from 0 to 1, where TCDD = 1. This toxicity rating is called the Toxic Equivalence Factor, or TEF. TEFs are consensus values and, because of the strong species dependence for toxicity, are listed separately for mammals, fish, and birds. TEFs for mammalian species are generally applicable to human risk calculations. The TEFs have been developed from detailed assessment of literature data to facilitate both risk assessment and regulatory control.[^\[19\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16829543-18) Many other compounds may also have dioxin-like properties, particularly non-ortho [PCBs](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCBs), some of which can have TEFs as high as 0.1.

The total dioxin toxic equivalence (TEQ) value expresses the toxicity as if the mixture were pure TCDD. The TEQ approach and current TEFs have been adopted internationally as the most appropriate way to estimate the potential health risks of mixture of dioxins. Recent data suggest that this type of linear scaling factor may not be the most appropriate treatment for complex mixtures of dioxins; further research into [non-linear](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear) toxicity models is required to substantiate this hypothesis.

Dioxins and other [persistent organic pollutants](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persistent_organic_pollutant) (POPs) are subject to the [Stockholm Convention](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Convention). The [treaty](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) obliges signatories to take measures to eliminate where possible, and minimize where not possible to eliminate, all sources of dioxin.

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=5)\] Health effects in humans
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dioxins build up primarily in fatty tissues over time ([bioaccumulate](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioaccumulate)), so even small exposures may eventually reach dangerous levels. In 1994, the US EPA reported that dioxins are a probable [carcinogen](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen), but noted that non-cancer effects (reproduction and sexual development, immune system) may pose an even greater threat to human health. [TCDD](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCDD), the most toxic of the dibenzodioxins, is classified as a Group 1 carcinogen by the [International Agency for Research on Cancer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer) (IARC). TCDD has a half-life of approximately 8 years in humans, although at high concentrations, the elimination rate is enhanced by metabolism.[^\[20\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid12107649-19) The health effects of dioxins are mediated by their action on a cellular receptor, the [aryl hydrocarbon receptor](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryl_hydrocarbon_receptor) (AhR).[^\[21\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16545780-20)

Exposure to high levels of dioxins in humans causes a severe form of persistent [acne](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acne_vulgaris), known as [chloracne](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloracne).[^\[22\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid11564625-21) A [case-control study](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-control_study) has shown an elevated risk of [sarcoma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcoma) (a type of cancer) associated with low-level exposure (4.2 [f](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femto)[g](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram)/m^3^) to dioxins from incineration plants.[^\[23\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-zambon2007-22) High levels of exposures to dioxins have been shown by epidemiological studies to lead to an increased risk of tumours at all sites.[^\[23\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-zambon2007-22) Other effects in humans may include:

-   Developmental abnormalities in the [enamel](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth_enamel) of children\'s [teeth](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teeth).^[\[24\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid15345345-23)[\[25\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid8260069-24)^

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=6)\] Health effects in other animals
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

While it has been difficult to establish specific health effects in humans due to the lack of controlled dose experiments, studies in animals have shown that dioxin causes a wide variety of toxic effects. In particular, TCDD has been shown to be [teratogenic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratogenic), [mutagenic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutagenic), [carcinogenic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenic), [immunotoxic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotoxic), and [hepatotoxic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatotoxic). Furthermore, alterations in multiple [endocrine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine) and [growth factor](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_factor) systems have been reported. The most sensitive effects, observed in multiple species, appear to be developmental, including effects on the developing [immune](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune), [nervous](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous), and [reproductive](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive) systems.[^\[32\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid10912242-31) These effects are caused at [body burdens](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_burden) close to those reported in humans.

Among the animals for which TCDD toxicity has been studied, there is strong evidence for the following effects:

-   Birth defects ([teratogenicity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratogenic))

> In rodents, including rats,[^\[33\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16835633-32) mice,[^\[34\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid10048156-33) hamsters and guinea pigs,[^\[35\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid17126467-34) birds,[^\[36\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid14522596-35) and fish.[^\[37\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16333842-36)

-   Cancer (including [neoplasms](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasms) in the mammalian lung, oral/nasal cavities, [thyroid](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid) and [adrenal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal) glands, and liver, [squamous cell](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamous_cell) carcinoma, and various animal [hepatocarcinomas](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatocellular_carcinoma))

> In rodents^[\[33\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16835633-32)[\[38\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid9061855-37)^ and fish[^\[39\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid10720744-38)

-   Hepatotoxicity (liver toxicity)

> In rodents,[^\[38\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid9061855-37) chickens,[^\[40\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid11489429-39) and fish[^\[41\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid14687977-40)

-   Endocrine disruption

> In rodents^\[[*citation\ needed*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]^ and fish[^\[42\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid16387744-41)

-   Immunosuppression

> In rodents[^\[43\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid10502532-42) and fish.[^\[44\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid3765346-43)

-   Learning[^\[32\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid10912242-31)

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polychlorinated_dibenzodioxins&action=edit&section=7)\] Studies of dioxins\' effects in Vietnam
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[US](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) veterans\' groups and [Vietnamese](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam) groups, including the Vietnamese government, have convened scientific studies to explore their belief that dioxins were responsible for a host of disorders, including tens of thousands of birth defects in children, that have affected Vietnam veterans as well as an estimated one million Vietnamese, due to their exposure during the [Vietnam War](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War) to [Agent Orange](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange), a defoliant chemical which was widely sprayed over Vietnamese land and which was found to be highly contaminated with TCDD. Several exposure studies showed that some US Vietnam Veterans who were exposed to Agent Orange had serum TCDD levels up to 600 ppt (parts per trillion) many years after they left Vietnam, compared to general population levels of approximately 1 to 2 ppt of TCDD. In Vietnam, TCDD levels up to 1,000,000 ppt have been found in soil and sediments from Agent Orange contaminated areas, three to four decades after spraying. In addition, elevated levels have been measured in food and wildlife in Vietnam.[^\[45\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-pmid7702115-44)

The most recent study, paid for by the [National Academy of Sciences](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), was released in an April 2003 report. This report is currently (March 2007) being revised for release again later in 2007.

The [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) found that dioxin levels in Vietnam veterans[^\[46\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioxin#cite_note-45) were in no way atypical when compared against the rest of the population. The only exception existed for those who directly handled Agent Orange. These were members of [Operation Ranch Hand](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ranch_Hand). Long-term studies of the members of Ranch Hand have thus far uncovered a possibility of elevated risks of diabetes.
