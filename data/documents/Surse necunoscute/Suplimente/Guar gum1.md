**Guar gum**, also called **guaran**, is a [galactomannan](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactomannan). It is primarily the ground [endosperm](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosperm) of [guar beans](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_bean). The guar seeds are dehusked, milled and screened to obtain the guar gum.[^\[1\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-0) It is typically produced as a free flowing, pale, off-white colored, coarse to fine ground powder.

![](media/image2.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.15625in"}\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=1)\] Production
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Guar gum is extracted from the guar bean, where it acts as a food and water store. The guar bean is principally grown in [India](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) and [Pakistan](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan), with smaller crops grown in the [U.S.](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.), [Australia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), [China](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China), and [Africa](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa). The drought-resistant guar bean can be eaten as a green bean, fed to [cattle](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), or used in [green manure](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_manure).

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=2)\] Trade
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

According to a [Reuters](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters) report India controls about 80 percent of the global trade in guar products.[^\[2\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-1). India exported 11 billion rupees worth of guar products in 2007/08 financial year ending March 2008. Pakistan trails India in the global trade. Industrial guar gum is the most sought after guar product and accounts for about 45 percent of the total demand. Industrial gum is used as a controlling agent in oil wells to facilitate easy drilling and prevent fluid loss.[^\[3\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-2)

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=3)\] Properties
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### \[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=4)\] Chemical composition

Chemically, guar gum is a [polysaccharide](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysaccharide) composed of the [sugars](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar) [galactose](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactose) and [mannose](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannose). The backbone is a linear chain of β 1,4-linked mannose residues to which galactose residues are 1,6-linked at every second mannose, forming short side-branches.

### \[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=5)\] Solubility and viscosity

Guar gum is more soluble than [locust bean gum](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust_bean_gum) and is a better emulsifier as it has more [galactose](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactose) branch points. Unlike locust bean gum, it is not self-gelling.[^\[4\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-LSBU-3) However, either [borax](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borax) or [calcium](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium) can [cross-link](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-link) guar gum, causing it to [gel](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel). In water it is [nonionic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonionic) and [hydrocolloidal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocolloid). It is not affected by ionic strength or [pH](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH), but will degrade at pH extremes at temperature (e.g. pH 3 at 50°C).[^\[4\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-LSBU-3) It remains stable in solution over pH range 5-7. Strong [acids](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid) cause [hydrolysis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolysis) and loss of viscosity, and alkalies in strong concentration also tend to reduce viscosity. It is insoluble in most hydrocarbon solvents.

Guar gum shows high low-shear [viscosity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) but is strongly shear-thinning. It is very [thixotropic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thixotropic) above concentration 1%, but below 0.3% the thixotropy is slight. It has much greater low-shear viscosity than that of locust bean gum, and also generally greater than that of other hydrocolloids. Guar gum shows [viscosity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) synergy with [xanthan gum](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthan_gum). Guar gum and [micellar casein](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micellar_casein) mixtures can be slightly thixotropic if a biphase system forms.^[\[5\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-4)[\[4\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-LSBU-3)^

### \[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=6)\] Thickening

Guar gum is economical because it has almost 8 times the water-thickening potency of [cornstarch](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornstarch) - only a very small quantity is needed for producing sufficient [viscosity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity). Thus it can be used in various multi-phase formulations: as an [emulsifier](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emulsifier) because it helps to prevent oil droplets from coalescing, and/or as a [stabilizer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28chemistry%29) because it helps to prevent solid particles from settling.

### \[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=7)\] Ice-crystal growth

Guar gum retards ice crystal growth non-specifically by slowing mass transfer across the solid/liquid interface. It shows good stability during freeze-thaw cycles.[^\[4\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-LSBU-3)

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=8)\] Grading
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Manufacturers define different grades and qualities of guar gum by the particle size, the viscosity that is generated with a given concentration, and the rate at which that viscosity develops. Coarse-mesh guar gums, will typically --- but not always --- develop viscosity more slowly. They may achieve a reasonably high viscosity, but will take longer to achieve. On the other hand, they will disperse better than fine-mesh, all conditions being equal. A finer mesh, like a 200 mesh, requires more effort to dissolve.[^\[6\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-5)

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=9)\] Industrial applications
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

-   [Textile](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) industry -- sizing, finishing and printing

-   [Paper](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper) industry -- improved sheet formation, folding and denser surface for printing

-   [Explosives](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive) industry -- as waterproofing agent mixed with [ammonium nitrate](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_nitrate), [nitroglycerin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroglycerin) etc.

-   [Pharmaceutical](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical) industry -- as binder or as disintegrator in tablets

-   [Cosmetics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetics) and toiletries industries -- thickener in [toothpastes](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toothpaste), conditioner in [shampoos](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shampoo) (usually in a chemically modified version)

-   Oil and gas drilling, [hydraulic fracturing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing)

-   [Mining](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining)

-   [Hydroseeding](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroseeding) -- formation of seed bearing \"guar tack\"[^\[7\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-6)

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=10)\] Food applications
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The largest market for guar gum is in the [food industry](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry). In the U.S., differing percentages are set for its allowable concentration in various food applications.[^\[8\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-7) In Europe, guar gum has [EU food additive code](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_food_additive_code) E412.

\[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Guar_gum&action=edit&section=11)\] Nutritional and medicinal effects
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[WikiProject Medicine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:WikiProject_Medicine) or the [Medicine Portal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal:Medicine) may be able to help recruit one.

Guar gum is a water-[soluble fiber](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble_fiber) that acts as a bulk forming [laxative](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laxative), and as such, it is claimed to be effective in promoting regular [bowel](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowel) movements and relieve [constipation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constipation) and chronic related functional bowel ailments such as [diverticulosis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diverticulosis), [Crohn\'s disease](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crohn%27s_disease), [colitis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colitis) and [irritable bowel syndrome](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irritable_bowel_syndrome), among others. The increased mass in the [intestines](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestine) stimulates the movement of waste and [toxins](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin) from the system, which is particularly helpful for good [colon](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_%28anatomy%29) health, because it speeds the removal of waste and [bacteria](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria) from the bowel and colon. In addition, because it is soluble, it is also able to [absorb](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Absorb_%28chemistry%29&action=edit&redlink=1) toxic substances (bacteria) that cause infective [diarrhea](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarrhea).

Several studies have found significant decreases in human serum [cholesterol](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol) levels following guar gum ingestion. These decreases are thought to be a function of its high soluble fiber content.

Guar gum has been considered of interest with regards to both weight loss and diabetic diets. It is a [thermogenic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermogenic) substance.[^\[10\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-9) Moreover, its low digestibility lends its use in recipes as a filler, which can help to provide [satiety](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satiety), or slow the digestion of a meal, thus lowering the [glycemic index](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index) of that meal. In the late 1980s, guar gum was used and heavily promoted in several weight loss products. The [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Food_and_Drug_Administration) eventually recalled these due to reports of esophageal blockage from insufficient fluid intake, after one brand alone caused at least 10 users to be hospitalized and a death.[^\[11\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-10) For this reason, guar gum is no longer approved for use in over-the-counter weight loss aids in the United States. Moreover, a [meta-analysis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis) that combined the results of 11 [randomized](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomize) controlled trials found that guar gum supplements were not effective in reducing body weight.[^\[12\]^](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum#cite_note-11)

Two Japanese studies using rats showed that guar gum supports increased absorption of [calcium](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium) occurring in the [colon](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_%28anatomy%29) instead of in the [small intestine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_intestine). This means that lesser amounts of calcium may be consumed in order to obtain its recommended minimum daily intake (RDI). This has obvious implications for reduced calorie diets, since calcium rich dairy products tend to be high in calories.

However, guar gum is also capable of reducing the absorbability of dietary minerals (other than calcium), when foods and/or [nutritional supplements](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutritional_supplement) containing them are consumed concomitantly with it. However, this is less of a concern with guar gum than with various nonsoluble dietary fibers.

Some studies have found guar gum to improve dietary [glucose](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose) tolerance. Research has revealed that the water soluble fiber in it may help people with [diabetes](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) by slowing the absorption of sugars by the small intestine. Although the rate of absorption is reduced the amount of sugar absorbed is the same overall. This helps diabetic patients by lowering the amount of insulin needed to keep the blood glucose at a normal level.

It also functions as an [adjuvant](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjuvant) for diabetic drugs that are sometimes employed for the treatment of noninsulin dependent diabetes. The effect is to help lower blood glucose levels. Thus, diabetic patients who are taking drugs should consult their doctors before supplementing with guar gum.
